A fictional example using three loads. No company data or live accounts.

LOAD 104

The balance looks right.
The billing is still wrong.

The same invoice arrived twice. An old payment update arrived late. Counting every update makes both totals wrong.

Why it matters: a matching balance could hide a duplicate bill or an incorrect payment record.

What I built

A check that counts the invoice once, uses the latest payment amount, and keeps the original records available to review.

The rule: check the invoice and payment separately before trusting the balance.

DELIVERY PAPERWORK

Arrival is one check.
Proof of delivery is another.

A truck at the destination does not mean the paperwork is complete. The billing team needs to know what is confirmed and what is missing.

Records available at 18:00 UTC in this example.

Delivery recorded
Proof of delivery checked
Invoice already exists

The rule: pause when evidence is missing. Keep a person in charge of sending invoices and customer messages.

THE TEAM'S NEXT MOVE

Fix the open items before going live.

The checks work. Two records still need attention, followed by a review against the original systems.

1

Confirm the invoice amount

Load 106 shows two different amounts. Accounting confirms the correct one; the developer updates the record.

Owner: Accounting + developer

2

Confirm which truck belongs to the load

One truck record has no confirmed match. Dispatch verifies it so its updates reach the right load.

Owner: Dispatch + developer

3

Review the results together

Compare with the original systems. Walk through the exceptions with the owner before turning on any live changes.

Owner: Project manager + accounting + dispatch

My role: keep each item assigned, make the blockers clear, and bring the owner a decision with evidence.

Start small. Make the work visible.

I would start with one fleet and one billing workflow, agree on what “done” means, and keep the existing team focused on the open items.
